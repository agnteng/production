# Thumbnail practice: sources and strength of evidence

Researched 2026-11-14. Most published thumbnail advice comes from tool
vendors and creator blogs and is opinion. Weigh a rule by its column here.

## Official (YouTube Help)

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| 16:9; 3840×2160 recommended, 640px minimum width; JPG or PNG; "as large as possible". Limit 2 MB from mobile, 50 MB from desktop. The Data API upload this repository uses is limited to 2 MB. | [Add video thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431) |
| Viewers usually see the thumbnail and title first. Consider the rule of thirds, keep the design from being too complex, use an easy-to-read font. Subscribers respond to familiar features; casual viewers to actions and emotions. | [Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300) |
| Test & Compare: up to three titles and thumbnails per video; the winner is the one with the highest watch time share, not CTR; runs up to two weeks; with no clear winner the first upload is kept; thumbnails under 1280×720 downscale the whole test to 480p. | [A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714) |
| Half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%; new or low-view videos range wider. Compare CTR over the long term and against your own videos. High CTR with low view duration may be clickbait; avoid it. | [Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154) |

## Reported data (vendor analyses; samples named, methods not published)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Of 500 breakout videos, about 69% showed a human face (80% of the top 50); 56% used high-contrast colour; 89% had a face, high contrast or both. About 1 in 20 used an exaggerated expression. Median overlay text: five words. | [vidIQ design tips](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/) |
| 2020 study of popular videos: 72% featured a person; 88% of 740 most-watched were vibrant in colour. Secondary report. | [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/youtube-thumbnails/) |
| One to three words can lift CTR and more than four usually lowers it. Cites analyses it does not link. | [vidIQ CTR guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-custom-thumbnails-ctr/) |

## Practitioner consensus (opinion, but several independent sources agree)

- The thumbnail adds a hook the title does not already state; build the two
  as a pair (vidIQ, Thematic).
- One focal point; three competing elements read as noise (vidIQ,
  ThumbnailTest).
- Text of three to five words, six at most; heavy sans-serif; a stroke,
  shadow or solid block behind it (vidIQ, Thematic, Buffer, ThumbnailTest).
- Text contrast of at least 4.5:1, the WCAG AA figure (vidIQ CTR guide).
- Keep text out of the bottom-right corner, under the duration badge, and
  off the face (vidIQ, Thematic).
- The face fills roughly a quarter to a third of the frame, is well lit, and
  sits away from the far edges; a genuine expression beats a forced one
  (vidIQ).
- Bright subject on a darker ground; crop tight; blur or mute the background
  (vidIQ, Thematic).
- Check at phone size, with a squint test, on a dim screen (vidIQ, Thematic).
- A repeatable template across a series: same fonts, palette and layout
  (vidIQ, Buffer, Thematic, ThumbnailTest).
- A still of a person should come from the same recording or the same day
  (Buffer).
- Change one element per variant when testing (vidIQ).

## Disputed or unsourced: don't cite these as fact

- Whether red works against YouTube's interface: vidIQ says avoid red, white
  and black; Thematic recommends red.
- "Good" CTR bands differ between every source; only YouTube's 2% to 10%
  range is official.
- "Viewers fixate on the thumbnail twice as long as the title", "consistency
  lifts clicks by 38%", "mobile feed renders at 160px", "90% of top videos
  use custom thumbnails": no primary source found.
- Nothing found that is specific to conference or meetup talks. The closest
  guidance is for podcasts and interviews: an emotive still from the
  recording, two fonts, two colours.
